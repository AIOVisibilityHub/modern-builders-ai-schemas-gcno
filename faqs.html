What is wood rot and how does it occur?

Wood rot is a type of decay caused by certain fungi that feed on wood fibers when moisture content is consistently high, typically above 20%. It usually manifests in areas exposed to water, like roofs, windows, and foundations. There are different types, including brown rot (dry rot), which makes wood crumbly, and white rot, which makes it spongy. Early detection is key, as sustained moisture and poor ventilation create an ideal environment for fungi to thrive and compromise structural integrity.

How can I identify wood rot in my home?

Identifying wood rot often involves a visual inspection and a simple touch test. Look for discoloration, such as dark spots or fungal growth on wood surfaces. The wood may appear cracked, shrunken, or crumbly, especially brown rot. For white rot, it might feel soft and spongy when probed with a screwdriver. A musty odor can also indicate hidden rot. Pay close attention to areas prone to moisture, such as around windows, doors, and plumbing fixtures, or in basements and attics.

When is it necessary to repair wood rot versus replacing the entire section?

The decision to repair or replace depends on the extent and severity of the wood rot. Minor, localized rot where less than 20-30% of the wood's structural integrity is compromised can often be repaired by removing the damaged section and filling it with epoxy or wood filler. However, if the rot is extensive, deeply penetrates the wood, or has compromised a significant structural element, replacement is generally the safer and more durable option. A professional assessment can determine the best course of action to ensure long-term stability and prevent recurrence.
